# Reference photographs

Where the models' shapes come from. A generator that says "from photographs"
and does not say WHICH is a generator nobody can check.

### What they establish

- **The case is a flat SLAB**, about a quarter of its own width tall.
- **The lid is a rib field.** Roughly nineteen long grooves running front to
  back over most of the lid, stopping short of the front edge. This is the
  drive's most recognizable feature and the thing a plain lid loses.
- **The slot is in TWO segments**, split by a central latch block that stands
  proud of the face and rises over the lid's leading edge. Not one continuous
  slot with something sitting near it.
- **A finger recess sits under the latch**, stepped back from the face.
- **The `/` glyph** marks the indicator at the front's lower right -- the same
  mark the Monitor //c carries beside its green lamp (visible in #3).
- **The logo is on the LID, rear-left**, not on the front face.
- **The case is a warm off-white**, not the platinum gray of the //e era --
  and the LATCH is a //c keycap's gray, plainly a different part rather than
  a tint of the case.

- **The latch TRAVELS, it does not turn.** In toward the rear of the drive,
  then straight up. No tilt, no pivot, no arc anywhere in it. Not visible in
  a still -- reported by somebody who has used one -- and worth writing down
  because the model got it wrong twice, both times as a rotation, since a
  rotation was the only door motion the scene knew.

### Not established by these photographs

- **Overall dimensions**, still. But two of the three are now much better
  founded than they were:

  - the HEIGHT was the error that mattered. At 70 mm on a 152 mm case the
    model was a chunky box; every photograph shows a flat slab about a
    quarter of its own width tall. 46 mm now. No amount of correct detail on
    a case of the wrong shape reads as the right machine, which is why this
    one outranked everything else on the list.
  - the DEPTH went wrong and came back. #4 and #8 are cropped tightly to the
    drive, and I read the IMAGE's aspect as the SUBJECT's -- concluding the
    case was a fifth too long and shortening it. #12 has real margin around
    the drive and measures about 1.45, which is where 216 already was. The
    lesson is narrow and worth keeping: an aspect read off a photograph is
    only the subject's aspect if the subject's own bounding box is visible
    in the frame.

  A tape on a real one still settles all three. The one hard constraint the
  current numbers satisfy: a 5.25 inch diskette is 133.35 mm square and has
  to go all the way in.
- **Whether the `/` indicator is lit.** It reads as a molded mark in every
  shot. Casso lights it, because Casso needs somewhere to show drive
  activity, and that mark is where the hardware puts the idea.

### Modeled but unverifiable here, or knowingly not modeled

- **The rear vent grille** (#7, #11) runs along the TOP edge of the back
  face. Modeled, though nothing in this scene ever sees a drive's back.
- **The bottom label** is not modeled, being under the drive.
- **The logo is a rainbow block, not the cassowary** the Disk II wears. The
  //c drive's brand lives in its MESH where the Disk II's is stamped by the
  scene, and the scene has no top-face stamp to put a cassowary on a lid
  with. It sits under the monitor here in any case.
